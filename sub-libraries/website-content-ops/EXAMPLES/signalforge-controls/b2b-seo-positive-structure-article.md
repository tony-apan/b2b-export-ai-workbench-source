1. **Actor:** A packaging retrofit engineer prepares one servo-axis readiness packet before candidate review.
2. **Trigger:** When a retrofit review begins with load, motion, supply, feedback, and acceptance assumptions split across owner documents, the engineer must reconcile one operating basis.

4. **Rework:** That evidence gap may force repeated sizing, drawing, and interface clarification across mechanical, electrical, and controls owners.
5. **Consequence:** The repeated review may delay a bounded design decision and allow weak assumptions to move toward sample planning.

## Use five decision blocks before opening a candidate review

### Confirm the operating load

Record the moving mass, transmission ratio and any vertical or counterbalanced condition. Estimates are acceptable only when labeled as estimates; an unlabeled assumption is a missing-evidence item.

### Describe the motion cycle

Separate index distance, move time, dwell, repetitions and emergency-stop behavior. A peak-speed value alone does not describe repeated duty.

### State the electrical boundary

Identify supply voltage, available current, braking method and cabinet constraints. This block determines what must be verified later; it does not prove compatibility.

### Map feedback and control interfaces

List the controller protocol, encoder or feedback type, I/O expectations and existing connector constraints. Unknown interfaces create a stop condition for candidate review, not permission to guess.

### Define the acceptance evidence

Name the measurements or tests that would show acceptable positioning, thermal behavior and fault recovery. Do not convert an unmeasured target into a guaranteed result.
